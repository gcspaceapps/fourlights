# The challenge: #11 Lunar Landing Site Navigator

Link: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/lunar-landing-site-navigator/

## In our words
Build a tool that lets mission planners, educators and the public compare lunar landing sites and dates quickly. For each site it should show where the Sun and Earth are relative to the site's own horizon, so you can judge how much solar power the site gets and when it can talk directly to Earth. The lunar south pole is the hard case: the Sun stays within a few degrees of the horizon, and the terrain decides everything.

The challenge's own wording: "an intuitive tool or application that lets mission planners, educators, and the public compare landing sites and dates quickly, visualizing Sun and Earth positions relative to the horizon to assess power generation potential and direct-to-Earth communication windows."

## What it asks for (checklist)
- [ ] Pick sites from a curated list (or custom coordinates) and compare them side by side
- [ ] Pick dates and times; show how conditions change across lunar days and seasons
- [ ] Sun and Earth positions relative to the **local** horizon; time to sunset and Earthset
- [ ] Metrics: solar elevation, sunlight duration, Earth visibility windows
- [ ] Visuals: 2D maps, 3D, animations or timelines, usable by planners and the public
- [ ] Use real LOLA topography for horizons (no atmosphere, so no refraction)
- [ ] Account for libration, steep south-pole terrain and permanent shadow

## NASA resources named by the challenge
JPL Horizons (or precomputed ephemerides), LOLA topography, NASA QuickMap, Moon Trek, and the CSA Moon Exploration portal.
