# Our image of the Moon's south limb (optional)

**What it is:** a timestamped telescope image of the Moon's south polar limb and terminator, taken from South East Queensland on Sat 14 Nov 2026 around 09:00 UTC (7pm AEST; Moon ~22% lit), to compare lit peaks with our prediction for that minute.
**Capture:** dedicated astronomy camera through a telescope; capture software and clock source recorded with the images. Location given as a coarse grid square only.
**Licence:** CC BY 4.0 (ours), published openly with raw frames and capture metadata before use. GPS stripped from the EXIF.
**Where it lives here:** to be decided when the images exist (no folder until then).

## What it gives us
A field check of the lighting model: which south-polar peaks and rims are lit at a known moment. Resolution from Earth is ~2 km at the Moon, so lit/unlit peaks only.

## If it's cloudy
A Stellarium view of the same time checks phase, libration and the Sun/Earth geometry (not terrain shadows). One timestamped LROC WAC image of the south pole is the stand-in for lit peaks (NASA data).

## Known flaws
Low altitude at dusk, poor seeing, weather; timestamps must be trusted (document the clock source).
